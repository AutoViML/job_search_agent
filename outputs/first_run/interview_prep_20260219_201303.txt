This approach gives me O(n) time complexity because I only iterate through the array once, and the hash map lookups are average O(1). The space complexity would be O(n) in the worst case to store all elements in the hash map."

      **Action:** Initially, I spent time trying to debug the framework directly, but it was too complex and time-consuming. I then decided to pivot. I researched alternative, simpler libraries for real-time communication that were known to be more stable. I communicated my findings to the team and, with their agreement, I quickly learned the basics of the new library and integrated it into our project. This involved understanding its API and adapting our existing data models.
      **Result:** We successfully integrated the new chat feature, and it remained stable throughout the hackathon. Our app was well-received, and we even won an award for best user experience. I learned the importance of quickly assessing complex problems, being adaptable, and not being afraid to change direction when a chosen path isn't working, even under pressure."

### 3. Company Research Guide

*(This section will be personalized for each company once provided.)*

**What to Research About [Company Name]:**

*   **Mission and Values:** What drives the company? What are their core principles?
*   **Products and Services:** Understand their main offerings, target audience, and how they make money.
*   **Recent News and Developments:** Look for recent press releases, product launches, funding rounds, or significant achievements.
*   **Technology Stack:** If possible, identify the programming languages, frameworks, and tools they commonly use. (Often hinted at in job descriptions or on their engineering blogs).
*   **Company Culture:** Read reviews on Glassdoor, LinkedIn, or their "About Us" page. Look for their approach to innovation, collaboration, and employee development.
*   **Competitors:** Who are their main rivals in the market?

**How to Demonstrate Culture Fit:**

*   **Align your language:** Use keywords from their mission statement or values in your answers.
*   **Share relevant experiences:** If they value collaboration, share a story about successful teamwork. If they emphasize innovation, talk about a time you proposed a new idea.
*   **Ask informed questions:** Your questions should show you've done your homework and are genuinely interested in their specific way of working.

**Intelligent Questions to Ask the Interviewer:**

*   "I saw that [Company Name] recently launched [specific product/feature]. Could you tell me a bit about the engineering challenges involved in bringing that to market?"
*   "What does a typical day look like for a Software Engineering Intern on this team?"
*   "How does the team handle code reviews and ensure code quality?"
*   "What opportunities are there for interns to contribute to design discussions or learn about different parts of the tech stack?"
*   "How does [Company Name] foster a culture of learning and professional development, especially for interns?"

### 4. General Interview Strategy

*   **Opening Statement Framework:**
    *   "Thank you for having me today. I'm very excited about the opportunity to discuss the Software Engineering Intern role at [Company Name]. I've been following [Company Name]'s work in [mention area] and am particularly interested in how my skills in [mention 1-2 key skills] could contribute to your team."
*   **How to Discuss Technical Projects:**
    *   **Use the XYZ Formula:** "I worked on X project where I accomplished Y by doing Z."
    *   **Focus on Impact:** Quantify your achievements whenever possible (e.g., "improved performance by 15%", "reduced bug reports by 10%").
    *   **Highlight Challenges and Solutions:** Discuss technical hurdles you overcame and the decisions you made.
    *   **Explain Your Role Clearly:** Differentiate your contributions from those of your team.
    *   **Be Honest:** Don't overstate your involvement. If you learned something new, say so.
*   **How to Discuss Soft Skills and Teamwork:**
    *   **Use the STAR Method:** As detailed in the behavioral questions section.
    *   **Be Specific:** Vague statements like "I'm a good team player" are less impactful than concrete examples.
    *   **Focus on Collaboration:** Describe how you actively listened, shared ideas, and supported teammates.
    *   **Showcase Conflict Resolution:** If discussing conflict, emphasize how you addressed it constructively.
*   **Closing Statement and Follow-Up Approach:**
    *   **Closing:** "Thank you again for your time. I've really enjoyed learning more about this role and [Company Name]. I'm very enthusiastic about the possibility of interning here and am confident I can make a positive contribution. Please let me know if you have any further questions."
    *   **Follow-Up:** Send a thank-you email within 24 hours to each interviewer. Reiterate your interest, briefly mention something specific you discussed, and reinforce your fit for the role.
*   **Salary Discussion Preparation:**
    *   **Research Intern Salaries:** Look up average intern pay for similar roles and locations on sites like Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, or through university career services.
    *   **Understand Intern Compensation:** Internships often have fixed pay scales. Be prepared for the company to state their range first.
    *   **If Asked Early:** You can defer by saying, "I'm more focused on finding the right opportunity and learning as much as possible during this internship. I'm confident we can agree on a fair compensation if we both feel it's a good match."
    *   **If Asked Later:** "Based on my research for Software Engineering Intern roles in [Location] and considering the responsibilities of this position, I'm looking for a range around [State your researched range, usually slightly above the midpoint]."
