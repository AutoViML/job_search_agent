<question>

Early testing revealed a slight binding issue in one of the joints. I adjusted the tolerances and chamfered the edges in my CAD model, then reprinted. The second iteration performed much better. Finally, I integrated a small DC motor with a custom gear reduction and a feedback encoder for precise positioning, which I then tested for actuation speed and grip force. **Result:** The final gripper design was 20% lighter than the previous one, achieved actuation in 0.4 seconds, and successfully handled all required objects during competition, contributing significantly to our team's performance. **D. Situational Questions**

<question>
  <type>Situational</type>
  <text>Imagine you're working on a critical hardware component, and you discover a potential design flaw that could impact performance or safety, but fixing it would cause a significant delay to the project deadline. How would you approach this situation?</text>
  <guidance>
    <evaluating>Your problem-solving skills, decision-making process, ethical considerations, and ability to communicate difficult news.</evaluating>
    <structure>1. Acknowledge the seriousness of the issue. 2. Outline your immediate steps for investigation. 3. Describe how you would communicate the problem and potential solutions. 4. Explain your decision-making process regarding the trade-offs.</structure>
    <key_points>
      - **Assessment:** Thoroughly investigate the flaw. Quantify its impact (performance degradation, safety risks).
      - **Information Gathering:** Understand the implications of a delay (market impact, other dependencies).
      - **Communication:** Proactively inform your manager and relevant stakeholders immediately.
      - **Solutioning:** Propose potential solutions, including:
          - A robust fix with a revised timeline.
          - A temporary workaround (if applicable and safe).
          - An analysis of risks associated with *not* fixing it.
      - **Collaboration:** Work with the team and management to make an informed decision.
      - **Prioritization:** Emphasize that safety and product integrity often trump immediate deadlines.
    </key_points>
    <example>
      "My first step would be to fully understand the nature and severity of the design flaw. I'd perform detailed analysis, simulations, and potentially build a quick proof-of-concept to quantify the impact on performance and, critically, safety. Once I have a clear picture, I would immediately inform my manager and the project lead, presenting them with the data. I would outline the potential risks associated with the flaw, especially if it relates to safety. Concurrently, I would work with my team to explore potential solutions: first, a robust fix, estimating the time and resources needed. If a full fix is infeasible within the deadline, I would investigate any safe, interim workarounds that could mitigate the risk, while still planning for the eventual permanent fix. My recommendation would always prioritize safety and product reliability. I would present these options, along with their pros and cons (including the impact of the delay versus the risk of shipping a flawed product), and collaboratively decide on the best course of action with management and relevant stakeholders."
    </avoid>
    Immediately deciding to ignore the flaw, panicking without a clear plan, or not communicating upwards promptly.
  </guidance>
</question>

### 4. General Interview Strategy

*   **Opening Statement Framework:**
    "Thank you for the opportunity to interview today. I'm [Your Name], and I'm very excited about the Product Design Engineer role at [Company Name]. My background in [mention 1-2 key areas like CAD, system design, prototyping] and my passion for [mention relevant industry/technology] make me particularly enthusiastic about this opportunity. I'm eager to learn more about how I can contribute to your team."
*   **Discussing Technical Projects:**
    *   **Be Specific:** Use concrete examples. Instead of "I worked on a project," say "For my capstone project, I designed a XYZ system that achieved ABC functionality."
    *   **Use the STAR Method:** For behavioral/situational questions related to projects, frame your answer using STAR.
    *   **Highlight Your Role:** Clearly define your specific contributions, especially if it was a team project.
    *   **Showcase Skills:** Naturally weave in the technical skills listed in the job description (CAD, simulation, prototyping, analysis, etc.).
    *   **Focus on Impact & Learning:** What was the outcome? What did you learn from the experience?
*   **Discussing Soft Skills and Teamwork:**
    *   **STAR Method is Key:** Use behavioral questions as an opportunity to showcase teamwork, problem-solving, communication, and leadership.
    *   **Active Listening:** In interviews, listen carefully to the question and the interviewer.
    *   **Positive Framing:** Discuss challenges as learning opportunities.
    *   **Show Enthusiasm:** Demonstrate genuine interest in collaboration and contributing to a team environment.
*   **Closing Statement and Follow-up:**
    *   **Closing Statement:** "Thank you for your time today. I've really enjoyed learning more about [specific aspect of the role/team/company discussed]. Based on our conversation, I'm even more confident that my skills in [mention 1-2 key skills] would be a great asset to your team. I'm very excited about the possibility of joining [Company Name]."
    *   **Follow-up:** Send a thank-you email within 24 hours. Reiterate your interest, briefly mention a key takeaway from the interview, and perhaps add a point you forgot to mention or elaborate on.
*   **Salary Discussion Preparation:**
    *   **Research:** Understand the market rate for the role and location. Sites like Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, and Levels.fyi can be helpful. For these roles, the provided salary ranges are very high and specific, so aim within or slightly above those stated ranges for your target.
    *   **Be Prepared to Defer:** For intern roles, salary is often less negotiable. If asked early on, you can say: "I'm more focused on finding the right opportunity and learning as much as possible during my internship. I'm confident we can discuss compensation further if we both feel it's a good mutual fit."
    *   **Know Your Value:** If you have unique skills or prior internship experience at top companies, that can be leverage.

    *   Teamwork/Collaboration
    *   Problem-Solving/Challenges
    *   Learning New Skills
    *   Leadership/Initiative
    *   Handling Failure/Mistakes
    *   Communication

    *   **Peer Practice:** Swap roles as interviewer and interviewee.
    *   **Alumni Network:** Leverage your university's alumni network for practice.
    *   **Online Platforms:** Consider platforms that offer mock interview services.
4.  **Company Research Reinforcement:** Regularly revisit company websites, news, and LinkedIn profiles to stay updated and identify new talking points.
5.  **Refine Your "Why":** Be crystal clear on why you want *this* role at *this* company.

By thoroughly preparing these areas, you'll be well-equipped to showcase your skills, enthusiasm, and potential for these exciting Product Design Engineer opportunities! Good luck!
